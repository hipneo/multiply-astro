Most founders ask about price before they've thought about scope. I understand why, budget is real, and nobody wants to get into a conversation without knowing if the numbers make sense at all.

But price without scope is meaningless. You wouldn't ask "how much does a developer cost?" and expect an answer that helps you make a decision.

That said, here's the answer to **how much does a fractional CMO cost** in 2026, so you can stop googling and start thinking about the right question.

**A fractional CMO costs $5,000 to $10,000 per month on a standard retainer, $2,500 to $4,000 per month for advisory-only, or $3,000 to $5,000 as a one-time diagnostic engagement. Hourly arrangements run $150 to $350 per hour but are not the standard model for ongoing work.**

**The short version:** the retainer is the main engagement and the number most founders are actually asking about. The diagnostic is a flat fee, not a monthly one, and the advisory tier sits below the retainer because the accountability is lighter. What you pay inside any of those depends entirely on [what a fractional CMO actually does](/blog/what-does-a-fractional-cmo-do/) for you week to week, because scope drives price. For a complete primer on [what a fractional CMO is](/blog/what-is-a-fractional-cmo/), including how the role differs from consultants, agencies, and interim hires, that pillar is the broader reference.

Here's exactly what you get in each model, and what moves the number inside it.

## The Four Ways Fractional CMOs Price

### 1. The Growth Diagnostic, $3,000–$5,000 flat

This is where most engagements start. It's a fixed-fee, time-bound project: I spend 2–3 weeks going deep on your business and come back with a 15–25 page marketing blueprint.

What's in it:
- Full audit of positioning, funnel, acquisition channels, content, analytics, and competitive landscape
- Customer interviews (5–8 of your best customers)
- Prioritised 90-day action plan tied to revenue targets
- Channel recommendations with rough budget ranges
- Team structure recommendations

This isn't a generic "recommendations deck." It's a working document you could hand to any competent marketing team and have them execute. Most clients tell me the diagnostic alone is worth more than they paid for it.

Who it's for: founders who want to understand the problem before committing to an ongoing engagement. Or founders who already have someone to execute but need the strategic architecture.

Note that this is a **flat fee, not a monthly rate**. It gets quoted alongside monthly numbers often enough that founders conflate the two and assume engagements start at $3,000 a month. They don't. The retainer floor is $5,000.

**$3,000–$5,000** depending on company stage, complexity, and how many channels I need to audit.

---

### 2. The Fractional CMO Retainer, $5,000–$10,000/month

This is the main engagement, and the number most people mean when they ask what a fractional CMO costs. Strategy plus hands-on execution, week by week.

Three tiers based on scope and hours:

**$5,000–$6,500/month, 2 days/week:**
Positioning, messaging, one primary acquisition channel, one major project per quarter. I attend key calls, make decisions, manage any external resources you have. Good for companies where the marketing machine is mostly absent and we're building from zero.

**$6,500–$8,500/month, 3 days/week:**
Full strategic ownership. Multiple channels running in parallel. I manage a small team of freelancers (copywriter, paid specialist, designer). Hands-on in campaigns, reporting, and iteration. This is where most clients land.

**$8,500–$10,000/month, 4 days/week:**
Near full-time involvement. Suits companies with bigger marketing budgets ($30K+/month in paid spend), multiple products or market segments, or a small in-house team that needs leadership. I act as the marketing P&L owner.

All tiers include: weekly status calls, Slack access, a monthly board-ready marketing report, and full ownership of the marketing function. You don't manage me, I manage the function and report to you.

---

### 3. The Advisory Retainer, $2,500–$4,000/month

This comes after the engine is built. You have a team running, campaigns live, the system working. You want a senior pair of eyes on a regular basis, not someone executing week to week, but someone who can catch drift, challenge assumptions, and help you make the bigger decisions.

2–4 hours per month. Monthly call, async review of key metrics, feedback on strategy and hiring decisions.

This is not the engagement to start with if your marketing is broken. It's the engagement to maintain once it's fixed. The most common expensive mistake in this whole pricing structure is hiring at the advisory tier and expecting retainer-level outcomes.

---

### 4. Hourly, $150–$350/hour

Hourly pricing exists, but it's not the standard model for serious fractional CMO work. You'll find it in two contexts: advisory arrangements where a founder wants access to senior judgment on an as-needed basis, and short-term project work with a defined output.

At the low end ($150–$200/hour), you're typically talking to someone with mid-level marketing experience, solid execution skills but not the strategic breadth of a genuine CMO. At $250–$350/hour, you're in the range where you'd find someone with real CMO-level operating history who is choosing to price hourly rather than by retainer.

The problem with hourly for this kind of work is the incentive structure. A fractional CMO on an hourly arrangement has no stake in efficiency. They're rewarded for time spent, not results produced. The best fractional CMOs I know don't offer hourly rates for ongoing work precisely because they don't want to be on the clock, they want to be on the outcome.

Hourly makes sense for one-off advisory calls, a specific deliverable with defined scope, or a one-time positioning review. It does not make sense as the primary structure for an ongoing engagement.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down

Six variables account for almost all the movement inside any of those ranges.

**Experience level.** A fractional CMO with ten years of operating experience at SaaS companies from $1M to $50M ARR charges more than someone who left a marketing manager role last quarter and went fractional. The difference isn't the title, it's pattern recognition. How many broken funnels have they fixed? How many positioning rewrites have they led? How fast can they diagnose your problem? That compression of time is what you're paying for.

**Scope of work.** Strategy plus execution plus team management costs more than strategy alone. Running three contractors, reviewing every piece of copy, managing a paid campaign and running a weekly founder sync is a different commitment from showing up monthly for a strategic session.

**Hours per week.** Simple arithmetic, but worth stating: the effective hourly rate for senior fractional CMOs is relatively consistent. What varies is how many hours you're buying and what they're pointed at.

**Number of channels running simultaneously.** Each channel is a system to build, instrument, and defend. Two channels is not twice the work of one, but it isn't the same work either.

**Industry and motion complexity.** A B2B SaaS selling to enterprise IT buyers on a six-month cycle with procurement review is more complex to market than a self-serve product with a seven-day trial. Complexity means more to understand, more channels to coordinate, more content per funnel stage. Enterprise GTM, multiple products or personas, and international markets all push the number up.

**Paid media budget under management.** More spend means more decisions, more risk, and more accountability.

Driving price **down**: early stage with one product, one ICP, one primary channel. An existing strong marketing generalist already executing, so you need leadership rather than implementation. A defined 90-day scope with a clear deliverable.

The cleanest engagements are the ones where scope is agreed upfront. "Build and run a demand gen engine for a B2B SaaS targeting mid-market HR teams" is a clear brief. "Help us with marketing" is not, and it tends to expand without an agreed ceiling.

## Fractional CMO Cost vs Agency Retainers

This comparison matters more than the full-time one for most founders, because most founders are already paying agencies. The real question is whether to add a fractional CMO on top of that spend or instead of it.

A mid-market SEO agency charges $5,000–$12,000/month. A paid media agency managing your LinkedIn or Google campaigns charges $3,000–$8,000/month plus a percentage of spend. A content agency producing eight articles a month charges $4,000–$8,000/month.

So you might already be spending $12,000–$25,000/month across agencies, each owning their lane, none of them connecting their work to your revenue outcome. Nobody asking whether the SEO articles are attracting the right buyer. Nobody questioning whether the paid campaign lands on a page that converts.

A fractional CMO at $7,500/month doesn't replace all of that. But they own the picture. They brief the agencies, hold them accountable, and redirect budget when one lane outperforms another. [Fractional marketing](/blog/fractional-marketing/) covers the agency comparison in full, including why an agency structurally cannot ask whether you need their service.

The comparison isn't fractional CMO versus agencies. It's fractional CMO plus right-sized agencies versus agencies with nobody pointing them in the right direction. The second option costs more and produces less.

If the comparison you're actually weighing is against a permanent hire, the [fractional CMO vs full-time CMO](/blog/fractional-cmo-vs-full-time-cmo/) breakdown runs the full-time numbers, the hiring timeline, the severance exposure, and the flexibility argument side by side. That's the better place for that math than here.

## Red Flags in Fractional CMO Pricing

**Too cheap, under $2,000/month:**

At this price, you're getting 3–5 hours of availability per month. That's not enough to own anything. It's a retainer for occasional advice, not a marketing leader. If someone is offering "fractional CMO services" for $1,500/month, they're either doing six of these concurrently (in which case your account gets 2–3 hours) or they're a mid-level marketer who hasn't found full-time work.

The one legitimate exception below $2,500 doesn't exist, and that's the point: the advisory tier starts at $2,500 precisely because that's the floor at which a senior operator can give you a real monthly review and still be reading your numbers between calls.

**Too expensive without clarity on scope:**

If someone quotes $15,000/month before they've understood your business, your ICP, or your current marketing state, walk. Price before scope means they're not thinking about fit. Unless you're post-Series A with significant budget to deploy, you don't need that price point anyway, and at it you're often paying for a brand-name operator primarily focused on the stage above yours. The best fractional CMOs for $1–5M ARR SaaS aren't the most expensive ones.

**Hourly for ongoing work:**

Covered above, but worth repeating in a list you might skim. Fractional CMOs who bill hourly for an ongoing engagement are not structured for accountability. You want someone on the outcome, not the clock.

**Performance-only deals:**

Some fractional CMOs offer to work purely on commission or revenue share. This sounds appealing until you realise that good fractional CMOs don't need to take payment risk, they have enough demand to work on fixed retainers. It also misaligns incentives: a performance-only model optimises for trackable attribution rather than the positioning and brand work that drives long-term growth.

## How to Think About ROI, Not Just Cost

The wrong frame: "can I afford $7,500 a month?" The right frame: "what's the return if this works?"

Here's the test. Your fractional CMO costs $7,500/month, which is $90,000/year.

**Scenario 1:** Your CAC is currently $4,200. A focused paid acquisition and funnel optimisation effort brings it to $3,000. If you close 50 customers per year, you just saved $60,000 in acquisition cost. That's most of the annual retainer right there, and the engine keeps running.

**Scenario 2:** You close one additional enterprise account at $36,000 ARR that came from better positioning and targeted outbound. That's 40% of the annual retainer paid back in a single deal.

**Scenario 3:** You've been spending $8,000/month on a content agency with nothing to show in pipeline. Stopping that spend and redirecting $4,000 into a well-run paid channel produces 15 qualified trials in 60 days. You've cut marketing waste and redirected the savings toward something that works.

The founders who get the most from these engagements treat them as an investment with a specific expected return. Before starting, we agree on what success looks like at 90 days, what the CAC target is, and what trial-to-paid rate would justify the spend. Those numbers exist, we can define them, and we measure against them. For the full framework on [measuring fractional CMO ROI](/blog/fractional-cmo-roi/), including the leading indicators to track in each of the first three months, that post breaks it down phase by phase.

The question isn't "can we afford a fractional CMO?" The question is what six more months of running marketing yourself costs in stalled growth, wasted spend, and your own time.

## FAQ

**How much does a fractional CMO cost per month?**

A fractional CMO retainer costs $5,000–$10,000 per month. At the lower end ($5,000–$6,500) you're getting roughly two days per week of strategic and leadership work with execution running through contractors. At the higher end ($8,500–$10,000) it's closer to four days per week with hands-on involvement in campaigns and team management. An advisory-only arrangement runs $2,500–$4,000 per month, and a one-time diagnostic is $3,000–$5,000 flat rather than monthly.

**How much does a fractional CMO cost per hour?**

Hourly rates run $150–$350 per hour. At $150–$200 you're typically talking to a mid-level marketer; $250–$350 is where genuine CMO-level operating history sits. But most fractional CMOs don't bill hourly for ongoing work, and if one insists on it, treat that as a signal. Hourly billing rewards time spent rather than results produced. For ballpark purposes, a monthly retainer divided by hours delivered works out to roughly $200–$400 per hour effective.

**What's the difference between fractional CMO rates and marketing agency fees?**

Agencies charge $5,000–$15,000 per month for one channel: SEO, paid media, content, or PR. A fractional CMO charges a similar range but owns the entire marketing function, sets strategy across all channels, manages the agencies and contractors, and is accountable for revenue outcomes rather than channel-specific outputs. Most founders spend more across multiple agencies in total and get less coordination than a single fractional CMO provides.

**How do I know if a fractional CMO's rates are fair?**

Fair rates are proportional to scope, hours, and experience. The red flags: under $2,000 per month (junior, or too little commitment to own anything), over $15,000 per month quoted before scope is defined, and hourly billing for ongoing work. The clearest signal of fair pricing is a fractional CMO who insists on understanding your business before quoting. Price should follow scope, not the other way around.

**Is a fractional CMO worth it for a startup?**

Depends on the stage. If you're pre-revenue or still finding product-market fit, no, a fractional CMO won't fix a product problem. If you have PMF, real revenue above $1M ARR, and growth that's stalled because marketing has no owner, then yes, it's almost certainly worth it. The ROI math works because you're buying CMO-level judgment at a fraction of the hiring cost, with zero equity dilution and no long-term commitment.

**What's the difference between a fractional CMO and a marketing consultant?**

A consultant tells you what to do. A fractional CMO does it, or manages the people doing it. The deliverable from a consultant is a document. The deliverable from a fractional CMO is a running marketing function with measurable results. Accountability is different too: a fractional CMO owns the number. A consultant owns the advice.

**How long do fractional CMO engagements typically last?**

Most serious engagements run 6–18 months. The first 30 days is diagnostic. Months 2–6 is building and launching. Months 6+ is optimising and either transitioning to a full-time hire, moving to advisory, or continuing fractional if that remains the right model. Short engagements of 1–3 months rarely produce meaningful results, the diagnostic alone takes 30 days, and you can't build a marketing engine in 60.
